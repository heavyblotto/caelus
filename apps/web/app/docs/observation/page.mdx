# Physical observation and forensics

The observation module turns ephemeris coordinates into local physical geometry at
a point on Earth: solar and lunar azimuth and apparent altitude with barometric
refraction, flat and sloped-ground 3D shadow vectors and sweep rates, optional
camera-relative glare angles, lens-independent lunar bright-limb orientation,
naked-eye planet visibility, diurnal transitions, and compact context headers.
Four functions expose this layer: `sceneGeometry` for forward scene modeling,
`verifyObservation` for checking claimed metadata and diagnosing discrepancies,
`chronolocate` for finding when a set of observations held at a known location,
and `geolocate` for inverting observations at a known timestamp to Earth
coordinates.

```ts run
import { Engine, julianDay, sceneGeometry } from "caelus";
import { embeddedData } from "caelus/data-embedded";

const engine = new Engine(embeddedData);
const jd = julianDay(2026, 6, 21, 18, 0, 0);

// New York City on the June solstice afternoon, camera aimed due south.
const scene = sceneGeometry(engine, jd, 40.7128, -74.006, {
  cameraAimAzDeg: 180,
  cameraAimAltDeg: 5,
  prevJdUt: julianDay(2026, 6, 20, 22, 48, 0),
  deadlineJdUt: julianDay(2026, 6, 21, 18, 45, 0),
});

({
  sunAltDeg: Number(scene.sun.altitudeApparentDeg.toFixed(2)),
  shadowBearingDeg: Number(scene.shadow.bearingDeg.toFixed(2)),
  shadowRatio: Number(scene.shadow.lengthRatio.toFixed(3)),
  glareRisk: scene.lighting?.glareRisk,
  moonClock: scene.moon.brightLimbClock,
  chronosense: scene.chronosense.line,
});
```

## Scene geometry and chronosense

`sceneGeometry(engine, jdUt, lat, lonEast, opts)` computes a complete local
physical snapshot at a UT Julian Day and observer location (longitude is east
positive, so the Americas are negative):

- **`sun`**: compass azimuth (`0` north, `90` east, `180` south, `270` west),
  true and refracted apparent altitude, Kasten-Young air mass, visual
  extinction, twilight stage (`day`, `civil`, `nautical`, `astronomical`,
  `night`), motion trend (`rising`, `culminating`, `setting`, `nadir`), angular
  rates per minute, and apparent angular diameter.
- **`shadow`**: 3D ray-plane intersection of a unit vertical pole onto flat
  ground or tilted terrain (`slopeDeg`, `slopeAzimuthDeg` pointing downhill).
  Reports `cast`, `selfShadowed` (when the Sun illuminates the back side of the
  slope), compass bearing, total and horizontal length ratios (`L/H`), compass
  sweep rate (`+` clockwise, `-` counterclockwise), length rate, and penumbra
  width.
- **`lighting`**: populated when `cameraAimAzDeg` is provided. Reports solar
  azimuth relative to the camera axis, screen-relative shadow angle, Sun-to-lens
  separation, and `glareRisk` (`direct`, `high`, `moderate`, `none`).
- **`moon`**: horizontal coordinates, illuminated fraction, phase name, waxing
  flag, elongation, visual magnitude, zenith-frame bright-limb angle
  (`0` right, `90` up, `180` left, `270` down) and clock label, plus moonlight
  shadow geometry when the Moon is bright and the sky is dark.
- **`visiblePlanets`**: classical planets above the apparent horizon with
  magnitudes and naked-eye flags evaluated against the twilight and Bortle sky
  limit.
- **`diurnal` and `chronosense`**: local sunrise, solar noon, and sunset Julian
  Days, minutes to the next horizon or civil-twilight transition, planetary
  hour, and a compact one-line `chronosense.line` header for injecting local
  diurnal and deadline state into agent prompts. Passing `prevJdUt` sets
  `nightCrossed` when local astronomical night elapsed between timestamps, and
  passing `deadlineJdUt` computes minutes remaining and `applying`, `exact`, or
  `separating` phase.

## Verifying observations and counterfactual diagnostics

`verifyObservation(engine, claim, observed)` checks any subset of physical
observables against a claimed timestamp and location. When `observed` is omitted
or empty, it returns `verdict: "unconstrained"`, `consistencyScore: null`,
empty `checks`, and the full `scene` object so callers can use one entry point
for both verification and ground-truth queries.

When constraints are supplied, every check reports its `status` (`consistent`,
`marginal`, `inconsistent`, `impossible`, or `indeterminate`), residual,
tolerance, and normalized residual:

- **Automatic uncertainty widening**: `timeToleranceMin` widens angle and shadow
  tolerances using each body's local motion rate, and `placeToleranceDeg`
  widens geometric tolerances for approximate coordinates.
- **Degenerate geometries**: when the Sun lies above 88 degrees altitude
  (near-zenith conditions such as Lahaina noon), shadow bearing is marked
  `indeterminate` rather than failing. Near full or new Moon (`illum > 0.97` or
  `< 0.03`), `moonBrightLimbDeg` and `moonWaxing` are also marked
  `indeterminate`.
- **Video shadow sweep**: `shadowSweep` accepts `{ dtMinutes, deltaBearingDeg }`
  and checks signed compass rotation over a time-lapse interval, separating
  Northern Hemisphere clockwise sweep from Southern Hemisphere counterclockwise
  sweep and flagging reversed footage.
- **Counterfactual forensics**: whenever a claim evaluates to `inconsistent` or
  `impossible`, `diagnostics` searches three alternative hypotheses and
  summarizes the best fit: a same-day clock or timezone offset (`timeShift`,
  capturing whole-hour and half-hour offsets such as UTC+5:30), an opposite
  latitude sign (`hemisphereFlip`), and a seasonal date offset at the same UTC
  clock time (`dateShift`).

```ts run
import { verifyObservation } from "caelus";

// Authentic June solstice scene in New York City.
const authentic = verifyObservation(
  engine,
  { jdUt: jd, lat: 40.7128, lonEast: -74.006 },
  {
    shadowBearingDeg: 41.75,
    shadowLengthRatio: 0.397,
    twilight: "day",
    moonIllum: 0.48,
    moonWaxing: true,
    shadowSweep: { dtMinutes: 30, deltaBearingDeg: 13.5 },
  },
);

// Same physical photo metadata misstamped by India's UTC+5:30 offset.
const spoofed = verifyObservation(
  engine,
  { jdUt: julianDay(2026, 3, 21, 10, 0, 0), lat: 28.6139, lonEast: 77.209 },
  { shadowBearingDeg: 302.11, shadowLengthRatio: 1.014 },
);

({
  authenticVerdict: authentic.verdict,
  authenticScore: Number(authentic.consistencyScore.toFixed(3)),
  spoofedVerdict: spoofed.verdict,
  recoveredOffsetMin: Math.round(spoofed.diagnostics.timeShift.offsetMinutes),
  summary: spoofed.diagnostics.summary,
});
```

## Chronolocation

`chronolocate(engine, place, observed, jdStart, jdEnd, opts)` searches a UTC
time range at a fixed location and returns every contiguous window in which all
constraints in `observed` hold simultaneously.

Before scanning intra-day steps, the solver computes analytical solar and lunar
altitude bounds and lunar illumination bounds for each day and prunes days where
the observations cannot occur (`daysPruned` vs. `daysScanned`). Surviving spans
are scanned on a fast per-day interpolated cache with narrow-peak rescue, window
edges are bisected to sub-second precision, and each window's peak timestamp
(`bestJd`) is refined by golden-section search.

```ts run
import { chronolocate, calendarDate } from "caelus";

const found = chronolocate(
  engine,
  { lat: 40.7128, lonEast: -74.006 },
  {
    shadowBearingDeg: 41.75,
    shadowLengthRatio: 0.397,
    moonIllum: 0.484,
    moonWaxing: true,
  },
  julianDay(2026, 6, 6, 18, 0, 0),
  julianDay(2026, 7, 6, 18, 0, 0),
);

const best = found.windows[0];
const stamp = calendarDate(best.bestJd);
({
  windows: found.windows.length,
  daysPruned: found.daysPruned,
  bestUtc: `${stamp.year}-${String(stamp.month).padStart(2, "0")}-${String(stamp.day).padStart(2, "0")} ${stamp.hour.toFixed(2)}h`,
  durationMin: Number(best.durationMinutes.toFixed(1)),
  fitScore: Number(best.fitScore.toFixed(3)),
});
```

## Geolocation

`geolocate(engine, jdUt, observed, opts)` solves the dual inverse problem: given
a known UTC timestamp and physical observations, return the subsolar and
sublunar points plus ranked feasible locations or regions on Earth.

Two solver paths are selected automatically:

- **Analytical fix (`method: "analytical"`)**: when terrain is flat
  (`slopeDeg === 0`) and the input supplies both solar direction
  (`sunAzimuthDeg` or `shadowBearingDeg`) and solar elevation
  (`sunAltitudeDeg` or `shadowLengthRatio`), `geolocate` inverts atmospheric
  refraction, solves the closed-form spherical celestial navigation roots,
  refines each root with damped Gauss-Newton iterations, and computes a Jacobian
  uncertainty `radiusKm` alongside `latRange` and `lonRange`.
- **Global spherical grid (`method: "grid"`)**: for all other observable
  combinations (such as Sun and Moon double altitude, shadow bearing combined
  with lunar bright-limb tilt, twilight plus visible planets, or sloped ground),
  equatorial coordinates and Greenwich apparent sidereal time are evaluated once
  at `jdUt`, a global spherical grid is swept in pure trigonometry, connected
  components are clustered across the antimeridian (`±180°` longitude), peaks
  are refined locally, and each region is classified as `point`, `arc`, or
  `band`.

```ts run
import { geolocate } from "caelus";

// Path A: closed-form analytical fix from a flat-ground shadow.
const fix = geolocate(engine, jd, {
  shadowBearingDeg: 41.7537,
  shadowLengthRatio: 0.3966,
});

// Path B: global grid search from Sun and Moon altitudes near Fiji.
const pacific = geolocate(engine, julianDay(2026, 6, 21, 2, 0, 0), {
  sunAltitudeDeg: 40.54,
  moonAltitudeDeg: 36.26,
  tolerances: { sunAltitudeDeg: 1.5, moonAltitudeDeg: 1.5 },
});

({
  analyticalMethod: fix.method,
  nycFix: [Number(fix.candidates[0].lat.toFixed(3)), Number(fix.candidates[0].lon.toFixed(3))],
  nycRadiusKm: Math.round(fix.candidates[0].radiusKm),
  gridMethod: pacific.method,
  fijiCandidate: [Number(pacific.candidates[0].lat.toFixed(2)), Number(pacific.candidates[0].lon.toFixed(2))],
});
```

## Through the MCP server

The `caelus-mcp` server exposes three corresponding tools over both stdio and
the hosted Streamable HTTP endpoint (`/api/mcp`):

- **`verify_observation`**: accepts `date` (UTC ISO, defaults to now), `lat`,
  `lon`, optional terrain, uncertainty, camera, and deadline fields, and an
  optional `observed` constraint object in snake_case. Calling it without
  `observed` returns the unconstrained ground-truth `scene` and `chronosense`
  header.
- **`chronolocate`**: accepts `lat`, `lon`, `start`, `end` (UTC ISO, up to 370
  days), `observed`, and optional terrain and step parameters, returning
  matching UTC windows with fit scores and pruning counts.
- **`geolocate`**: accepts `date` (UTC ISO), `observed`, and optional search
  bounds (`lat_min`, `lat_max`, `lon_min`, `lon_max`), returning `method`,
  `subsolar` and `sublunar` coordinates, and ranked candidate regions.

All three tools live inside `caelus-mcp/core` with zero Node dependencies so
they run identically in Node, browsers, and Cloudflare Workers.

## Validation and limits

- **Python and TypeScript parity**: `packages/caelus/test/observe-golden.test.ts`
  verifies 28 cases (1,927 individual field checks across mid-latitude,
  equatorial, polar midnight sun, polar night, sloped terrain, chronolocation,
  and analytical and grid geolocation) against `python/astroengine/observe.py`
  with a worst numerical difference of `6.71e-7`.
- **Empirical spoof benchmark**: `packages/caelus/test/observe.test.ts` tests
  120 global scenarios (60 authentic scenes with realistic measurement noise and
  60 spoofed scenes covering whole-hour and half-hour timezone shifts,
  hemisphere flips, seasonal date shifts, impossible night shadows or lunar
  visibility, and reversed video shadow sweeps). All 120 classify cleanly
  (precision `1.000`, recall `1.000`, `0` false-impossible verdicts on authentic
  scenes, and `40/40` counterfactual diagnostic recoveries).
- **Terrain and atmosphere limits**: sloped shadows model a single planar tilt
  at the observer rather than a digital elevation model or surrounding mountain
  ridges. Refraction uses a standard exponential barometric pressure profile
  from `elevationM` without local temperature inversions, which matters mainly
  within two degrees of the horizon.
- **Measurement uncertainty**: photograph lens distortion, non-vertical poles,
  compass magnetic declination (all bearings in Caelus are relative to true
  north), and cloud diffusion introduce real-world error. Supply realistic
  per-field `tolerances` or `timeToleranceMin` when evaluating noisy imagery.
