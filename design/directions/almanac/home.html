Dating a sky from the signs of its planets

Placeholder results from a daily engine.position() scan. dateSky() is not in caelus 0.25.0 and these controls are not wired.

Open the instrument

Sky dating takes a partial description of a sky and lists every date that fits it. Scholars date ancient horoscopes the same way: a text that names the sign of each planet can be matched against the computed sky for every day in the supported span.

Saturn is in Aries during 405 separate windows between 3000 BCE and 3000 CE. Requiring Jupiter in Leo leaves 107 of them, and Mars in Leo leaves 34. With the Sun, Venus, Mercury, and the Moon also fixed by sign, 3 windows remain, and tonight is one of them.

How it is checked

Measured accuracy, body by body

CI checks the engine in two stages. A Python reference engine is first calibrated against Swiss Ephemeris, and the TypeScript port is then replayed against 3,440 golden checks derived from it. The worst deviation recorded across that suite is 0.61 nano-arcsecond, which sits far below any astronomically relevant scale, so a porting bug fails the build instead of reaching a release.

The table gives the position error of each body over the wide band. Per-body notes, the event timings, and the house-system comparisons are on Validation, and the bugs this suite has caught are written up in the Build Notes.
