# B2 review supplement (transits)

Read `pipeline/reviewer-instructions.md` first; everything there applies
(the four adversarial tests, wording-level repairs only, never touch
id/family/when/atomIds, self-check with check-texts.mjs on exactly the
files you edited). This sheet adds the transit-specific failure modes to
hunt for.

1. **Register drift.** A transit entry describes a period, not a person.
   Flag any entry that reads as a character portrait (natal register) or
   that promises an outcome for the period instead of describing its
   weather.
2. **The arc rule** (voice-sheet rule 9). Every transit-aspect entry must
   address the applying/exact/separating arc and, for every transiting
   body except the Sun, the possibility of retrograde re-hits. Flag
   entries that skip either, and entries that recite them in the same
   sentence shape as a neighbor (formula).
3. **Selector overreach.** The selector guarantees the transiting body,
   the natal body (or house, or the station direction), and the aspect.
   Flag any claim about the natal body's sign or house, the reader's age,
   generation, or life stage, or a specific calendar duration ("about
   three weeks in March"). Tempo facts derivable from the transiting
   body's own cycle are fine; check them against the body (a Saturn pass
   colors months, not days; a Moon house transit is days, not weeks).
4. **Cycle claims.** Return rhythms must be right: Saturn square rhythm
   roughly seven years, conjunction about twenty-nine; Jupiter conjunction
   about twelve; Uranus opposition near the middle of a long life, the
   conjunction once at most; Neptune and Pluto aspects once at most for
   most points. Flag age claims ("your Saturn return at 29" is a rhythm
   fact stated as an age only for same-body returns, where it is
   guaranteed; for anything else, flag it).
5. **Doom and medical drift.** Hard transits (Saturn, Pluto, Mars) invite
   verdict language and health speculation. The non-fatalistic rule and
   the no-directives rule hold at full weight; "pressure you can work
   with" framing, never "loss is coming".
