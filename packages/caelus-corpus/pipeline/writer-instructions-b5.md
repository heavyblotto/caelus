# B5 writer slice — instructions (lots, receptions, stars, parallels, the Vedic layer)

You are writing one slice of the Caelus interpretation corpus
(`caelus-corpus`), batch B5: the condition layer — calculated points,
chart mechanics, fixed stars, declination, and the Vedic families.
Everything in `pipeline/writer-instructions.md` applies (read it first,
along with `pipeline/voice-sheet.md` and `editorial/editorial-voice.md`);
this sheet adds the rules specific to these families.

## What a B5 entry is

B1 read placements, B2 the moving sky, B3 two charts, B4 time. A B5
entry reads a **condition or a calculated point**: a lot cast from three
points of the chart, a reception or dispositor binding two planets'
agendas, a star standing behind a natal body, two bodies sharing a
declination, or the sidereal layer a Vedic reading starts from. The
reader may never have met the technique: the first sentence or two may
say plainly what the thing is, in ordinary words, before the meaning
starts.

## Family-specific rules

**lot-sign / lot-house** (200–500 words). A lot is a calculated point:
an arc taken between two places in the chart and cast from the
Ascendant, so it concentrates a theme at one degree without any body
standing there. Each of the seven Hermetic lots has a keeper and a
subject: Fortune (the Moon's lot — body, circumstance, livelihood, what
comes to you), Spirit (the Sun's — deliberate action, career, what you
set in motion), Eros (Venus's — desire and what draws you), Necessity
(Mercury's — constraint, the non-negotiable), Courage (Mars's — nerve
and fight), Victory (Jupiter's — success, backing, faith), Nemesis
(Saturn's — the buried, the reckoning, the hard edge). The selector
guarantees the lot and its sign, or the lot and its house — never both,
never the sect of the chart (the computation flips between day and
night births, but your essay does not know which the reader has), and
never any body's position. Nemesis essays carry the non-fatalism rule
hardest: a reckoning point, not a doom point.

**dispositor** (150–400 words). The named planet sits in its own sign
and so answers to no other planet: chains of rulership end at it. The
essay reads that anchoring — one function in the chart that is
self-supplied, a place other agendas resolve to. Guaranteed: the body,
and that it is a final dispositor. Not guaranteed: which sign of its
domiciles, the house, or how many chains lead in.

**reception** (150–400 words). Two planets each sit in a sign the other
rules: a standing exchange of hospitality between two functions. Route
cells (by domicile, by exaltation, mixed) guarantee only the dignity
route — write the character of that route, naming no planets. Body
cells guarantee only that the named planet is in a mutual reception —
write what it is like when that function has a standing ally, naming no
partner and no signs.

**star** (200–500 words). One of your natal bodies stands conjunct the
named star, by ecliptic longitude within a tight orb. The essay is the
star's: its constellation seat, its character in the tradition
(Ptolemy, Robson, Brady — researched, then written fresh), and what it
means for a point of a chart to carry it. Guaranteed: the star, and
that some natal body sits on it. Not guaranteed: which body, the sign
(a star's zodiac degree drifts with precession — name the
constellation, not the sign), or the house. Non-fatalism holds
especially for the difficult stars: Algol is intensity and appetite
survived, not decapitation.

**star-contact** (150–400 words). The named body on the named star —
shorter and more pointed than the star essay, which stays the place for
the star's full portrait. Write the meeting: this function of the
chart, colored by this star's character. Do not restate the star
essay's material at length; the two can render side by side.

**parallel** (150–400 words). The two named bodies hold the same
declination on the same side of the celestial equator — a quiet
alignment the tradition reads like a conjunction that does not show in
longitude (Sepharial's point: a position, not an aspect). Write the
blend of the two planets as a standing undertone. Guaranteed: the pair
and that the relation is a parallel. Not guaranteed: any longitude
aspect between them — never imply they are also conjunct — and not the
contraparallel, which these cells do not cover.

**nakshatra-moon** (250–600 words). The Moon in one of the 27 sidereal
lunar mansions — the seat of Vedic natal reading. Each mansion's span
and its Vimshottari lord are fixed doctrine and safe to state (the
lords cycle Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rahu, Jupiter, Saturn,
Mercury, three times around). Write the mansion's character: its
symbol, its deity where the tradition gives one, the temperament of a
Moon placed there. Gloss Sanskrit on first use; do not pile on
terminology. Sidereal means the mansion does not translate to a
tropical sign claim — never name the reader's tropical Moon sign.

**nakshatra-pada** (120–300 words). The quarter of the mansion — a
chip, not a second essay. Each pada maps to one navamsa sign, which is
fixed doctrine and safe to use as the pada's flavor. Write what the
quarter sharpens or softens in the mansion's character; do not restate
the mansion essay, which renders beside it. With 108 siblings, this
family has the corpus's highest skeleton risk: vary the opening move,
vary the structure, and keep each pada's one distinguishing note
concrete.

**varga-d9** (200–500 words). The named body in the named sign of the
navamsa, the ninth divisional chart — the fruit of the placement, read
in the tradition for marriage, dharma, and what a life ripens into.
The sign named is the navamsa sign, sidereal. Guaranteed: body,
division, navamsa sign. Not guaranteed: the rasi (birth-chart) sign,
the navamsa house, or any dignity — a body may sit anywhere in D1 and
still hold this D9 sign.

**varga-frame** (150–400 words). How to read one divisional chart: what
the division covers (D2 wealth and resources, D3 siblings and courage,
D9 marriage and dharma, D10 career and public action, D12 parents and
lineage, D30 adversity and what tests you), how it is cast in one plain
sentence, and what weight the tradition gives it. The cell fires
whenever the engine computed that division, so the essay explains the
lens and claims no placement in it.

**yoga** (200–500 words). A named classical combination the engine
detected. The definition is guaranteed by detection and safe to state:
the five Mahapurusha yogas (Ruchaka/Mars, Bhadra/Mercury, Hamsa/Jupiter,
Malavya/Venus, Shasha/Saturn — the planet in its own or exaltation sign
and in a kendra, a whole-sign angle from the Ascendant), Gajakesari
(Jupiter in a kendra from the Moon), Budha-Aditya (Sun and Mercury in
one sign), Chandra-Mangala (Moon and Mars in one sign), Kemadruma (the
Moon isolated — no planet conjunct it or in the signs either side).
Write the yoga's promise as the tradition frames it, hedged as the
tradition's report; never grade its strength, never invoke
cancellations, and never issue the classical verdicts as fact —
Kemadruma is self-reliance and an unaccompanied mind, not poverty
foretold.

## Facts you may rely on (and no others)

The doctrine stated above (lot keepers, mansion lords and spans,
pada–navamsa mapping, varga subjects, yoga definitions) is guaranteed.
The selector guarantees exactly its fields. It never guarantees: the
sect of the chart, the sign or house of anything the selector does not
name, tropical positions for the sidereal families, the reader's age,
era, gender, family shape, occupation, or means. Hedge biography;
degender partners and caretakers; no promises, no financial or medical
directives.
