# B3 adversarial review — supplement

Everything in `pipeline/reviewer-instructions.md` applies. This sheet
adds the traps specific to B3 (synastry aspects, house overlays,
composite placements).

## B3-specific checks

1. **Compatibility verdicts.** The batch's governing rule: contact is
   not compatibility. Flag and repair any sentence that grades a
   relationship, ranks a contact as good or bad synastry, predicts
   whether a bond lasts, or advises staying, leaving, pursuing, or
   avoiding. Hard contacts must read as friction that is also traction;
   soft contacts as ease that can also be inertia.

2. **Assumed relationship type.** No entry may assume romance,
   marriage, cohabitation, exclusivity, or children unless the cell
   names that ground. The other person is "they", never gendered, never
   "your partner" outside partnership cells. Parent, sibling, friend,
   and colleague readings must stay available.

3. **Direction.** Synastry aspect cells are ordered pairs. Verify each
   entry is written from the reader's side and would NOT serve as the
   reverse cell's text: an essay that reads identically both ways has
   lost the fact the selector guarantees. Check the mirrored cell in
   the other slice for accidental symmetry.

4. **Overlay two-sidedness.** Overlay entries must say what the other
   person experiences with the reader's body in that house, not only
   what the reader gets. They must not add "the same is true in
   reverse" — that view is a separate surface.

5. **Composite voice.** Composite entries are about the relationship as
   a third thing: "the two of you", "this bond", not "you". Flag any
   composite entry that has drifted back into second-person natal
   voice, and any that implies the composite chart forecasts duration.

6. **Selector overreach.** Aspect cells know two bodies and an aspect,
   nothing about signs, houses, or orbs. Overlay cells know one body
   and one house. Composite cells know one body and one sign.

7. **Shared skeletons.** 500 aspect essays across 34 slices is the
   largest single family in the corpus: check hard for repeated opening
   moves, repeated "X meets Y" scaffolding, and duplicated closers,
   both inside a slice and against the neighboring slices for the same
   body.
